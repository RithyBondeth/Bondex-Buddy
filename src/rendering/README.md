# Rendering

`habitat.ts` renders a 110 × 100 Canvas at 2×, with image smoothing disabled. The character remains on its integer pixel grid. Source artwork is cropped into a maximum 48 × 48 body and normalized to the shared 16-color palette and binary alpha. Original PNGs remain untouched.

`motion.ts` samples slow breathing and reading/sipping poses. Happy has one two-second choreography: anticipation, extension, a nine-pixel ballistic hop, landing compression, a smaller four-pixel hop, and rest. Grounded poses keep their bottom edge at the baseline. Wardrobe anchors follow the same breathing deformation and jump translation. The grounded shadow narrows during a hop.

`life.ts` supplies independent secondary gestures: brief blinks, occasional double blinks, an ear flick, tail swishes, and curious glances. A deterministic twelve-second window varies their timing across cycles. Sleeping and TeaBreak keep their own calm poses and breathing rather than inheriting upright gestures. The Idle face has open highlighted eyes that close during a blink. Pointer position is used transiently to ease the Idle head toward a nearby pointer; it is never recorded. Reduced motion renders a neutral, static pose without pointer tracking or particles.

The renderer caches both articulated sprites (up to 120) and assembled frames (up to 240). Each assembled frame has an alpha mask; identical silhouettes share a mask reference, so native updates happen only when the interactive silhouette changes. Pixel readback happens only on a cache miss. Decor remains stationary while the character and wardrobe animate. Decorative steam, hearts, and sleep glyphs never intercept clicks.

`AnimationClock` caps rendering at 60 fps with cadence correction for different display refresh rates. It cancels its RAF loop while hidden or when reduced motion is requested. Rendering consumes mood state; it does not decide focus, break, or progression rules.

Open `/motion-review.html` on the development server for the interactive pet studio. Click Ember for the greeting, choose a mood to inspect it, and preview cosmetics without changing saved progress. The expandable motion timeline supports pause, restart, and scrubbing. Its renderer checks exercise animated silhouettes, accessories, caching, and reduced motion. Native transparency, Dock occlusion, pointer pass-through, placement, and drag behavior require checks in Tauri on each target OS. Native focus events keep hidden controls from intercepting clicks after the user returns to another app.

`playful.ts` loads three distinct eight-frame sheets: the quadruped walk, a compact gallop (`run-cycle-v1.png`), and whole-body play poses (`play-cycle-v1.png`). Running uses gathered and extended suspension, rather than speeding up the walking artwork. Running frames register at the ears so tucked paws retain their airborne clearance without the head jumping vertically. All sheets keep a common scale within the cycle, integer pixels, the existing palette, and binary alpha. Wardrobe follows each frame's detected head anchors.

A pounce takes 1.5 seconds: play bow, crouch, hind-paw takeoff, tucked flight, forepaw contact, landing compression, recovery, and a curious head tilt. Its jump arc starts after anticipation and settles before recovery. Hiding uses the crouch; peeking holds the head-tilt pose. Play pauses use a standing frame.

The native controller advances paws from eased distance traveled: 48 logical desktop pixels per walking stride and 84 per running stride. Progress/rate updates let the renderer follow acceleration and braking. Native position updates use finer steps during excursions while retaining lower-frequency idle polling. Reduced motion disables both gait and travel.

`src-tauri/src/roaming.rs` runs short, eased excursions on the current display, followed by a play bow/pounce, a slower Dock approach, and two brief peeks. Concealment places the fox inside the Dock inset rather than far beyond the screen. A peek rises for 550 ms and then holds, before ducking and reemerging facing the next run. It infers the Dock/taskbar edge from the monitor work area; an auto-hidden Dock falls back to the display edge. The existing floating window stays below the macOS Dock layer. The controller uses native window positions, including the brief offscreen part of a hide, and brings the pet back after each finite excursion. No Dock settings or permissions change. It refreshes monitor geometry, respects reduced motion and focus sessions, and stops while the user approaches the pet. Dragging, **Rest here**, and **Show spirit** restore a reachable resting position; **Play now · run and peek** restarts play. Play mode takes precedence over automatic idle naps; **Rest here** restores the normal sitting or sleeping mood. Play preferences are session-only.

The pet studio's **Watch Ember play** previews the full run–pounce–approach–hide–peek–return sequence behind an opaque Dock illustration. Timeline cues let reviewers freeze the run, pounce, hiding, and peek poses. Actual Dock occlusion requires the desktop app. The renderer fixture also verifies both directions and all outfits against their click masks.

These are procedural articulations of the existing illustrations. They are not hand-authored sprite sheets; approved animation sheets can replace them without changing the engine.
